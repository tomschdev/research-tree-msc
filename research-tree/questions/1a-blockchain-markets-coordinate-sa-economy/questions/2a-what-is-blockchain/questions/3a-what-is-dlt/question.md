## Rationale


Blockchain has no standard technical definition [@Narayanan2017Pedigree], but blockchain systems
are commonly treated as a subset of distributed ledger technology (DLT). 2a fixes what
"blockchain" means in this MSc. To do that, it needs the general class to which blockchain
belongs, and that class's necessary properties. Those properties are the minimal set against which
a later gain is called "blockchain-specific" rather than "digitisation". This question settles
what a DLT system is, which properties are necessary for it, and how it is structured.

What each candidate conclusion means here:

- **converged**: peer-reviewed or research-centre accounts of DLT (at least Rauchs et al. 2018,
  Kannengießer et al. 2020 and Ballandies et al. 2021) agree on a common set of necessary
  properties. Some sources may omit a property or name it differently, but none rejects one as
  unnecessary. That set is adopted as the working definition.
- **contested**: at least one of these sources, or another verified one, rejects or materially
  redefines a property the others treat as necessary. Whether permissioned ledgers count as DLT
  is an example. The Finding names the competing accounts and states which is adopted and why.
